# Governance

SmartCities currently uses a **founding maintainer model** while public product contracts are being established.

## Principles

Governance optimizes for public technical decisions, evidence over status, citizen safety and privacy, explicit human authority, interoperability, open standards, reversible increments, contributor stewardship, and independence from any single vendor, municipality, political administration, or sponsor.

## Current model

The repository owner acts as founding maintainer and final steward of merge decisions during foundation. Material architecture should be discussed publicly whenever security, privacy, legal, contractual, or personal-data constraints do not require confidentiality.

## Decision levels

Local reversible work can proceed through normal review. Cross-cutting public contracts, identity, privacy, GIS conventions, decision traces, human authority, Criterio integration, localization architecture, Rust boundaries, and compatibility guarantees should have a design issue or ADR before broad implementation.

Licensing, DCO, Code of Conduct, security policy, governance, trademarks, and transfer to a neutral organization require explicit maintainer approval and repository documentation.

## Human authority

Software may inform civic decisions. It does not acquire democratic, legal, administrative, engineering, or professional authority merely by computing a result. Human-authority boundaries are architecture.

## Political independence

SmartCities may support public consultation, policy evaluation, infrastructure planning, and government operations. Project governance does not endorse parties, candidates, administrations, or campaigns.

## Path to broader governance

Multiple sustained maintainers, independent adoption, recurring external contributions, ecosystem dependencies, or a need for neutral stewardship should trigger evaluation of documented maintainer areas, consensus rules, trademark stewardship, and potentially a neutral foundation.

## No purchased authority

Sponsorship, procurement, employment, or vendor relationships do not automatically grant technical governance authority.

## Amendments

Governance changes require a pull request describing the problem, effect, and transition implications.
